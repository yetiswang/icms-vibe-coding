Act 2 · Scene 5

A harness, in three legs.

🛠
Tools
Search the web. Edit a file. Run a shell command. Call an API. Open a browser.
The model decides which tool to call, with what arguments.
📁
Files
It reads and writes the real filesystem. Your repo, your notes, your assets.
Context becomes everything you've already written. Output becomes a saved artifact.
🔁
Loops
It runs a step, reads the output, decides what to do next, runs again.
A bug pops up; it tries a fix. The build fails; it reads the log and retries.

Tools + Files + Loops = an agent that can actually finish the task,
not just describe how you'd do it.

Act 3 · Scene 7

The workflow, in three moves.

01
Read
Point Claude at your existing assets.
Templates, slides, notes, screenshots.
It learns your visual style, vocabulary, and audience by reading what you've already made. No "design system" doc needed.
02
Repo
Make a GitHub repo. Init, commit, push.
One command, one source of truth.
Versioned, shareable, auto-deployable to GitHub Pages. Every change is a commit you can roll back.
03
Vibe
Describe what you want, in plain words.
Watch it appear. Ask for changes.
"Make the title bigger." "Use the supramolecular image." "Add a section for collaborators." It edits, refreshes, you react.

Act 4 · Scene 12 · Second brain

An Obsidian vault the AI can read.

01
Capture
Daily notes, meeting notes, paper highlights, half-finished thoughts. Plain markdown files. Yours forever, in any text editor.
02
Link
Wikilinks tie ideas together. Tags, backlinks, the graph view. A wiki of your own thinking emerges over time.
03
Query
Claude reads the vault as context. Answers grounded in what you've already written. Your wiki, your voice, your domain.

AI is only as good as the context you give it.
Markdown is the lingua franca. Your notes become a tool the AI can read.

Inspired by Andrej Karpathy's writings on personal AI knowledge systems. I keep a vault myself, won't show its contents (privacy), happy to chat one-on-one.
